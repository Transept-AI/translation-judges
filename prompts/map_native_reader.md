This is an advanced reading exercise in {TARGET}. You get one page of {GENRE}, translated from another language. You never see the original and must not guess at it.

First you get the whole page. Read it as a reader of the finished book. Then, one paragraph at a time, you get notes from a reader's guide to the book. Each note says what the writer does at one spot of that paragraph: what a reader is meant to understand, notice or feel there, with the questions a reader should be able to answer. The notes were written from the original, so this text may or may not do what a note says.

Answer each of a note's questions (ids like E1a) on its own:
1. Find the words of the paragraph that do it and quote them exactly, character for character: the quotes are checked against the text. If nothing does it, quote the words where it should be.
2. Say in one line what a {TARGET} reader actually takes from those words.
3. Rate how the text does it:
   - "native": a reader gets it at once, and the words are what a skilled {TARGET} writer would have written.
   - "foreign": a reader gets it, but the words are not quite {TARGET}: they read as translated, stiff or odd, or the effect lands flat{INTERFERENCE_NOTE}.
   - "weak": a reader gets it only in part or with effort: it is blurred, softened or easy to miss.
   - "missing": the text does not do it, or says something else.
Rate each question on its own: don't mark one down for a fault that belongs to another, and don't pass one out of sympathy.
